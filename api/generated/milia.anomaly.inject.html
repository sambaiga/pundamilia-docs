milia.anomaly.inject#

Synthetic anomalies to inject into otherwise normal profiles.

Real labelled anomalies are rare, so detectors are evaluated by injecting known faults into held-out normal days. Every injector takes one profile (a 1-D array of readings, typically one day) and a numpy generator, and returns a modified copy. Default ranges scale with the profile length and reproduce the anomaly chapter’s settings exactly for 48-step (half-hourly) days.

Break the export (negative) readings into several short episodes at random times.

The exported values are split, in order, into non-overlapping episodes placed at random positions, so the total export is kept and only its timing changes. A profile without export is returned unchanged.

  • rng (Generator) – Random generator.

ndarray

milia.anomaly.inject.tripping(profile, generator, trips=((2, 5), (1, 4)))#

An inverter that trips repeatedly within the export window of one day.

On its over-voltage or anti-islanding protection the inverter stops exporting for short spells. During each spell the PV output is lost, so the meter records the household’s demand, taken as the median of the day’s import readings, since the demand under the array is not observed. Unlike fragmented(), the energy of the spells is lost, not moved, and no export appears outside the export window.

Parameters:

  • profile (ArrayLike) – Net-load readings of one day, negative while exporting.

  • generator (Generator) – Random generator.

  • trips (tuple[tuple[int, int], tuple[int, int]]) – [low, high) number of trips and [low, high) length of each in steps.

Return type:

ndarray

Returns:

A modified copy; a day without export is returned unchanged.

Examples

>>> day = np.full(48, 0.5)
>>> day[20:30] = -2.0
>>> tripped = tripping(day, np.random.default_rng(0))
>>> bool((tripped[:20] == day[:20]).all() and (tripped[20:30] > 0).any())
True
milia.anomaly.inject.clockerror(days, day, shift=20)#

The readings a meter records for one day when its clock runs shift steps behind local time.

A meter that records UTC as local time labels every reading early by the time-zone offset, so the readings of a recorded day are those of local times shift steps later, partly from the following day. The fault needs consecutive days, so it is not a one-day Injector.

Parameters:

  • days (ArrayLike) – Readings of consecutive days, shape (days, steps).

  • day (int) – The day whose recorded readings are replaced; it must not be the last day.

  • shift (int) – Offset in steps.

Return type:

ndarray

Returns:

The readings recorded for day.

Raises:

ValueError – If day is the last day, whose later readings are not available.

Examples

>>> days = np.arange(12.0).reshape(3, 4)
>>> clockerror(days, 0, shift=1).tolist()
[1.0, 2.0, 3.0, 4.0]
milia.anomaly.inject.clockshift(profile, generator=None, shift=20)#

A day recorded by a meter whose clock runs shift steps behind local time, from the day’s own readings.

clockerror() needs the readings of the following day. Where days are evaluated one at a time, as by selfbaseline(), the recorded day is approximated by rolling the day’s readings, which is exact when consecutive days are alike: a midday export then appears shift steps earlier, in the night, while the energy and the shape of the day are unchanged.

Parameters:

  • profile (ArrayLike) – Readings of one day.

  • generator (Generator | None) – Unused; present so every injector shares a signature.

  • shift (int) – Offset in steps; 20 half-hours is ten hours, the offset of UTC from the time of Victoria.

Return type:

ndarray

Returns:

The recorded day.

Examples

>>> clockshift(np.arange(6.0), shift=2).tolist()
[2.0, 3.0, 4.0, 5.0, 0.0, 1.0]
milia.anomaly.inject.adopt(cube, adopters, technologies, generator, pv, sessions, kwp=(3.0, 8.0), onset=0)#

Add a technology to the readings of each adopter, from a given day to the end of the period.

PV subtracts an array of random size times the output per kWp, so the readings become a net load that is negative while the household exports. Level 2 charging adds a vehicle’s recorded sessions; Level 1 adds them scaled to 2.4 kW.

Parameters:

  • cube (ArrayLike) – Readings in kW, shape (meters, days, steps).

  • adopters (ArrayLike) – Indices of the adopting meters.

  • technologies (list[str]) – One of ADOPTIONS per adopter.

  • generator (Generator) – Random generator, for the vehicle and the array size.

  • pv (ArrayLike) – PV output per kWp, shape (days, steps).

  • sessions (ArrayLike) – Recorded charging of a session pool in kW, shape (vehicles, days, steps).

  • kwp (tuple[float, float]) – Range of array sizes in kWp.

  • onset (int) – First day with the technology installed.

Return type:

ndarray

Returns:

A modified copy of the readings.

Raises:

ValueError – If a technology is not one of ADOPTIONS.

Examples

>>> cube, pool = np.ones((2, 3, 4)), np.full((1, 3, 4), 7.0)
>>> adopted = adopt(cube, [0], ["ev2"], np.random.default_rng(0), np.zeros((3, 4)), pool, onset=1)
>>> adopted[0, 0].tolist(), adopted[0, 1].tolist()
([1.0, 1.0, 1.0, 1.0], [8.0, 8.0, 8.0, 8.0])

milia.anomaly.inject.behaviourfault(days, kind, fault, count, generator)#

Inject a behaviour fault into randomly chosen days on which the behaviour occurs.

For export (net load, negative while exporting), "fragmented" is tripping() and "offhours" is clockerror(), the physical faults of an inverter and of a meter clock. For charging (power drawn by a charger), they split a session into fragments (fragmented()) or move it in time (offhours()), as a faulty charger or a misconfigured timer would.

Parameters:

  • days (ArrayLike) – Readings of consecutive days, shape (days, steps).

  • kind (str) – "export" or "charging".

  • fault (str) – One of BEHAVIOURFAULTS.

  • count (int) – Number of days to corrupt; fewer if fewer days show the behaviour.

  • generator (Generator) – Random generator.

Return type:

tuple[ndarray, ndarray]

Returns:

The corrupted copy and a boolean label per day.

Raises:

ValueError – If kind or fault is unknown.

Examples

>>> days = np.full((6, 48), 0.5)
>>> days[:, 20:30] = -2.0
>>> corrupted, labels = behaviourfault(days, "export", "offhours", 2, np.random.default_rng(0))
>>> int(labels.sum()), bool((corrupted[labels][:, :8] < 0).any())
(2, True)
milia.anomaly.inject.meterfault(cube, meters, fault, generator, days=30, resolution=0.001, interval=0.5)#

Inject one fault into the readings of chosen meters.

A stuck register or a dropout affects days random days of each meter. Under-recording hides half of every reading: the stored readings are already rounded to the register’s resolution, so the energy lost to that rounding is restored with a uniform dither within one step before the share is hidden and the register rounds again; halving the rounded readings directly would create ties that no real register produces.

Parameters:

  • cube (ArrayLike) – Power in kW, shape (meters, days, steps).

  • meters (ArrayLike) – Indices of the meters to corrupt.

  • fault (str) – One of METERFAULTS.

  • generator (Generator) – Random generator.

  • days (int) – Days corrupted per meter for a stuck register or a dropout.

  • resolution (float) – The register’s resolution in kWh.

  • interval (float) – Length of a step in hours.

Return type:

ndarray

Returns:

A corrupted copy of the cube.

Raises:

ValueError – If fault is unknown.

Examples

>>> cube = np.full((2, 40, 48), 0.6)
>>> faulty = meterfault(cube, [1], "underreport", np.random.default_rng(0))
>>> round(float(faulty[0].mean()), 2), round(float(faulty[1].mean()), 2)
(0.6, 0.3)
