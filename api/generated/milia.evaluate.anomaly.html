milia.evaluate.anomaly#

Evaluating detectors by injection against each entity’s own normal baseline.

The protocol, for each entity (a meter with many days of readings):

  1. Split its days into training, calibration, held-out normal and an anomaly pool.

  2. Fit the detector on the training days and score every other day.

  3. Inject known anomalies into pool days and score them too.

  4. Calibrate a conformal threshold on the calibration days, then report the false-positive rate on held-out normal days, the recall on injected days, and the threshold-free AUC.

selfbaseline() produces every score once; summarize() turns them into metrics at any alpha without refitting, so an alpha sweep is cheap.

milia.evaluate.anomaly.selfbaseline(entities, detector, featurize=None, injections=None, sets=None, count=5)#

Score calibration, held-out normal and injected days for every entity.

Each entity gets its own detector, fitted on its own training days, so the baseline is the entity’s normal behaviour rather than the population’s. Injection seeds are derived from the injection, entity, repeat and day, so results reproduce across processes.

Parameters:

  • entities (Mapping[Hashable, ArrayLike] | ArrayLike) – Per-entity (days, steps) arrays, as a mapping or a (entities, days, steps) array.

  • detector (BaseDetector) – Unfitted detector, cloned per entity.

  • featurize (Callable[[ndarray], ndarray] | None) – Maps one day’s readings to a feature vector; identity when omitted.

  • injections (Mapping[str, Callable[..., ndarray]] | None) – Name to injector; INJECTIONS when omitted.

  • sets (dict[str, ndarray] | None) – Day split from split(); a default split when omitted.

  • count (int) – Injected days per anomaly type per entity.

Return type:

DataFrame

Returns:

One row per scored day – entity, set ("cal", "normal" or the injection name), day and score (higher = more anomalous).

Examples

>>> from milia.anomaly.ensemble import members
>>> from milia.data import profiles, synthetic
>>> cube = profiles.tensor(synthetic.generate(meters=2, days=60, steps=24).pivot)
>>> sets = split(60, sizes=(30, 15, 8))
>>> results = selfbaseline(cube, members(["ecod"])[0][1], sets=sets, count=2)
>>> sorted(results["set"].unique())
['cal', 'drift', 'dropout', 'normal', 'stuck']

milia.evaluate.anomaly.balance(head, meters, imports, generation, demand, blocks, hours, sizes, kind='underreport', models=('head', 'components'), alpha=0.1, steps=48, step=0.5, contaminate=0)#

Test the energy balance of a transformer block by block, with one meter tampering in the held-out block.

For each held-out block (a week, say), the loss model (lossmodel()) is fitted and the conformal threshold of the daily unexplained energy calibrated on the other blocks; the meter then tampers throughout the held-out block (tamper()). A size of zero measures the false-alarm rate. Holding out a whole block, rather than random days, tests a threshold calibrated on other seasons, as an operator’s would be. With contaminate, the meter also tampers, at the same size, in that many calibration blocks, as it would if it had tampered before the operator calibrated.

Parameters:

  • head (ArrayLike) – Head power per step, whole days in time order.

  • meters (ArrayLike) – Summed meter readings per step.

  • imports (ArrayLike) – Summed import register per step.

  • generation (ArrayLike) – Summed generation per step.

  • demand (ArrayLike) – The tampering meter’s demand per step.

  • blocks (ArrayLike) – Block label of each step; each block is held out in turn.

  • hours (ArrayLike) – Hour of the day of each step.

  • sizes (Sequence[float]) – Sizes of the tampering, as tamper() reads them.

  • kind (str) – One of TAMPERS.

  • models (Sequence[str]) – Loss models compared, from LOSSMODELS and LOSSALTERNATIVES.

  • alpha (float) – Target false-alarm rate of the daily test.

  • steps (int) – Steps per day.

  • step (float) – Length of a step in hours.

  • contaminate (int) – Calibration blocks in which the meter also tampers (the first ones after the held-out block, cyclically).

Return type:

DataFrame

Returns:

One row per held-out block, size and model – block, size, model, flagged (the share of the block’s days whose unexplained energy exceeds the threshold) and days (their number).

Examples

>>> rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
>>> days, steps = 28, 48
>>> imports = 20 + 10 * np.sin(np.linspace(0, 2 * np.pi * days, days * steps)) + rng.normal(0, 1, days * steps)
>>> head = imports + 0.002 * imports**2 + rng.normal(0, 0.05, imports.size)
>>> hours = np.tile(np.arange(steps) / 2, days)
>>> blocks = np.repeat(np.arange(4), 7 * steps)
>>> result = balance(head, imports, imports, np.zeros_like(imports), imports / 10, blocks, hours, [0.0, 0.5])
>>> result.groupby("size")["flagged"].mean().round(2).tolist()
[0.07, 1.0]

milia.evaluate.anomaly.behaviour(series, kind, training, count=5, faults=('fragmented', 'offhours'), representations=('magnitude', 'events', 'eventscircular'), seed=0, detector='iforest', pooled=False)#

Compare representations of a day for detecting behaviour faults, each entity against its own past.

For each entity, an Isolation Forest is fitted on its first training days in each representation, faults are injected into count later days that show the behaviour (behaviourfault()), and the AUC of the forest’s scores on the later days is recorded.

Parameters:

  • series (ArrayLike) – Readings per entity, shape (entities, days, steps).

  • kind (str) – "export" or "charging".

  • training (int) – Number of leading days each entity’s detector is fitted on.

  • count (int) – Days corrupted per entity and fault.

  • faults (Sequence[str]) – Faults injected, from BEHAVIOURFAULTS.

  • representations (Sequence[str]) – Representations compared, from REPRESENTATIONS.

  • seed (int) – Seed of the injections and the forests.

  • detector (str) – "iforest" (an Isolation Forest, which isolates points by axis-parallel splits and needs no scaling) or "lof" (the local outlier factor on standardised features, which compares a point’s density with that of its neighbours); comparing the two separates the effect of the representation from that of the detector.

  • pooled (bool) – Fit one detector on the training days of every entity, rather than one per entity (a global against a local model).

Return type:

DataFrame

Returns:

One row per entity, fault and representation – entity, fault, representation and auc. Entities with fewer than two corrupted days, or none left normal, are skipped.

Examples

>>> hours = np.arange(48) / 2
>>> rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
>>> clear = np.clip(np.sin(np.pi * (hours - 7) / 10), 0, None) * 4.0
>>> series = 0.5 + rng.normal(0, 0.02, (2, 80, 48)) - rng.uniform(0.4, 1.0, (2, 80, 1)) * clear
>>> result = behaviour(series, "export", training=50, count=5, faults=("offhours",))
>>> sorted(result["representation"].unique())
['events', 'eventscircular', 'magnitude']

milia.evaluate.anomaly.adoption(cube, labels, pv, sessions, shares, config, mix=('ev1', 'ev2', 'pv', 'pvev'), alpha=0.1, holdout=0.3, seed=0)#

Share of meters still described by a segmentation, and still trusted, as a growing share adopts PV or EVs.

Each meter is described by its profile features (config). The segments’ centroids are fitted on most meters and two split-conformal thresholds calibrated on the rest: the distance to the nearest centroid, which defines the membership sets (ConformalSets), and the distance to the nearest meter of the reference, the trust radius (KNNRetriever). Adoption (adopt()) then moves the adopters away from the reference.

Parameters:

  • cube (ArrayLike) – Readings in kW, shape (meters, days, steps), from 1 January.

  • labels (ArrayLike) – Each meter’s segment.

  • pv (ArrayLike) – PV output per kWp, shape (days, steps).

  • sessions (ArrayLike) – Recorded charging of a session pool in kW, shape (vehicles, days, steps).

  • shares (Sequence[float]) – Shares of meters that adopt.

  • config (ProfileConfig) – The profile features describing a meter.

  • mix (Sequence[str]) – Technologies drawn for the adopters, uniformly; one entry gives every adopter the same technology.

  • alpha (float) – Miscoverage level of both thresholds.

  • holdout (float) – Share of meters held out to calibrate.

  • seed (int) – Seed of the split, the adopters, their technologies and their arrays and vehicles.

Return type:

DataFrame

Returns:

One row per share – share; described, the share of meters whose set holds a segment, near 1 - alpha before adoption; adopterdescribed, the same among adopters; single; empty; and trusted, the share within the trust radius. A meter whose profile features are not finite (a day whose maximum is zero cannot be peak-normalised) lies within no threshold, so it is neither described nor trusted.

Examples

>>> rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
>>> cube = rng.uniform(0.2, 2.0, (30, 366, 48))
>>> config = profilefeatures.ProfileConfig(normalized=True, period="seasonal", hemisphere="south")
>>> pv, pool = np.zeros((366, 48)), np.zeros((2, 366, 48))
>>> result = adoption(cube, np.repeat([0, 1], 15), pv, pool, [0.0], config)
>>> list(result.columns)
['share', 'described', 'adopterdescribed', 'single', 'empty', 'trusted']

milia.evaluate.anomaly.monitor(cube, labels, pv, sessions, share, onset, mix=('ev1', 'ev2', 'pv', 'pvev'), baseline=16, drift=0.5, alpha=0.1, holdout=0.3, seed=0, statistic='cusum', nominal=None)#

Detect, week by week, the meters that stop resembling their segment after adopting a technology.

Each segment’s centroid of a week is the mean weekly profile (weekly()) of its meters before adoption, so the distance of a meter to its segment carries no seasonal trend common to the segment. A one-sided CUSUM of each meter’s weekly distance (paths()) is compared with a limit calibrated on held-out meters without adoption (pathlimit()), because real meters drift from their segment through the year and the CUSUM’s nominal limit would alarm many that do not adopt. A share of the other meters adopts on day onset.

Parameters:

  • cube (ArrayLike) – Readings in kW, shape (meters, days, steps), from 1 January.

  • labels (ArrayLike) – Each meter’s segment.

  • pv (ArrayLike) – PV output per kWp, shape (days, steps).

  • sessions (ArrayLike) – Recorded charging of a session pool in kW, shape (vehicles, days, steps).

  • share (float) – Share of the monitored meters that adopt.

  • onset (int) – Day on which the adopters install their technology.

  • mix (Sequence[str]) – Technologies drawn for the adopters, uniformly.

  • baseline (int) – Initial weeks that define each meter’s normal distance; must end before onset.

  • drift (float) – CUSUM allowance in standard deviations.

  • alpha (float) – Target share of non-adopting meters alarmed within the horizon.

  • holdout (float) – Share of meters held out, without adoption, to calibrate the limit.

  • seed (int) – Seed of the split, the adopters and their technologies.

  • statistic (str) – "cusum" (paths()) or "ewma" (ewmapaths()).

  • nominal (float | None) – A fixed decision limit to compare with the calibrated one; when given, frame also holds nominalalarm, the week of the first alarm at that limit.

Return type:

Monitoring

Returns:

The Monitoring.

Raises:

ValueError – If the baseline does not end before the onset.

Examples

>>> rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
>>> cube = rng.uniform(0.2, 2.0, (20, 140, 48))
>>> pv, pool = np.zeros((140, 48)), np.full((2, 140, 48), 7.0)
>>> result = monitor(cube, np.repeat([0, 1], 10), pv, pool, share=0.5, onset=84, mix=("ev2",), baseline=8)
>>> len(result.frame), result.statistic.shape[1]
(14, 12)
milia.evaluate.anomaly.pointadjust(flagged, segments)#

Point adjustment: a true anomalous segment counts as found in full if any one of its readings is flagged.

The adjustment is common in benchmarks of time-series detectors and inflates their scores, since a single lucky flag in a long segment credits the whole segment; it is reported only to show that inflation.

Parameters:

  • flagged (ArrayLike) – One boolean per reading.

  • segments (Sequence[tuple[int, int]]) – (start, stop) indices of each true anomalous segment.

Return type:

ndarray

Returns:

The adjusted flags.

Examples

>>> pointadjust([False, True, False, False], [(0, 3)]).tolist()
[True, True, True, False]
